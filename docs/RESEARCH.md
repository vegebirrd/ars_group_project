# Research design

## Motivation

Streaming makes music more accessible, but discovery remains costly. Critics can draw attention to an artist by conferring recognition or provoking controversy; whether attention becomes recurring listening is a separate question. The project studies Pitchfork as a cultural intermediary and asks who benefits from its coverage.

## RQ1 · Valence, attention and persistence

**Question.** How are review scores and evaluative extremity related to short-run attention, its persistence and subsequent music consumption?

**Predictions.** Highly favorable scores may signal aesthetic quality; unusually negative scores may attract curiosity. The latter mechanism suggests a possible U shape, which is tested rather than assumed. Sustained attention and chart visibility are examined separately from immediate information seeking.

**Main outcomes.** Relative Wikipedia traffic change, additional daily pageviews, abnormal attention relative to a pre-review trend, and Spotify/YouTube chart visibility. Estimate short (0–6), middle (7–27) and longer (28–55) windows. Compare each with the week preceding publication (−7 to −1) and a quieter baseline (−28 to −8).

**Design.** Event-level regressions of attention on score, its square where appropriate, baseline attention, pretrend, year and release timing. Display full event-time trajectories and U-shape endpoint tests; use matched high/low-score comparisons as a complementary descriptive check.

## RQ2 · Heterogeneous visibility and inequality

**Question.** Does cultural criticism help lesser-known artists become visible, or does it concentrate attention among already-established artists?

**Predictions.** Smaller artists may exhibit larger proportional changes, while established artists can gain more additional daily visitors. Genre and past critical recognition may shift the relationship.

**Prior recognition.** Use three separately interpretable, strictly pre-review measures: Wikipedia daily views from days −180 to −61; prior Pitchfork coverage and recognition dated before day −57; and historical Spotify/YouTube chart participation over 52 weeks ending at least eight weeks before publication.

**Design.** Interact review score with continuous pre-review visibility and genre; report the score association at low, median and high baseline popularity. Show proportional log changes and absolute traffic increments side by side. Keep history-based popularity and contemporaneous baseline distinct.

## Complementary evidence

The text module describes professional review language (tone, intensity, innovation / commercialism vocabulary) and public YouTube discussion. It extends the two questions rather than creating a third predictive task. The consumption analysis follows album/artist chart presence but cannot observe units sold outside licensed sources.

## Interpretation

The observed comparison is between differently reviewed events, not between reviewed and unreviewed albums. Pre-review trajectories and nearby album releases are central to interpreting what part of the observed change may precede review publication. The project reports conditional associations and evaluates plausible attention and signaling mechanisms.
